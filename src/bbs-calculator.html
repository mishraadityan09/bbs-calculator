Help — how every number works (tap to open)

Cutting length method
Cutting length = shape length + hooks − bend deductions + laps, then rounded UP to the next 25 mm (IS 2502 Cl. 5.1.1 — toggle in Settings).
• Shape length = sum of outer dimensions A/B/C/D for the chosen shape (formulas listed under "Shape library" below).
• Hooks: each hook adds hook factor × dia, minimum the hook floor. Default 10d ≥ 75 mm = legacy seismic 135° hook (IS 13920:1993 / common bridge practice). IS 13920:2016 allows 6d ≥ 65 mm; non-seismic 90° stirrup hook is 8d — change the factor in Settings if your spec says so.
• Bend deductions (because outer dimensions overlap at each corner): 45°→1d, 90°→2d, 135°→3d, 180°→4d (SP-34 site convention, sharp-edge method). Each shape applies its own bend counts automatically; the Custom shape lets you set them by hand.
• Laps: leave the Laps cell blank and the tool auto-inserts laps when the bar is longer than the stock length: pieces = 1 + ⌈(L − stock)/(stock − lap)⌉. Auto values show with a *. Type a number to override. Lap length = lap factor × dia.
📐 Tutorial — "find the total bar length in Section C-C" (the full problem)
This is the complete workflow from drawing to answer — the way the prefilled table was built.

Step 1 — Get the geometry from the dimension sheet (PDF page 1). Section C-C itself shows no sizes; View B-B and the plan give them: cap cross-section at the pier = 3400 wide × 2500 deep (the "3.4 m × 2.5 m"), cap length = 11,250 + 2,900 + 11,250 = 25,400. The notes give clear cover 45 mm, lap 35d (50d for mark 1), M60/Fe500D.

Step 2 — List the bars that Section C-C cuts (PDF page 6). The reinforcement table gives mark, dia, spacing and counts — but NO lengths (that's the work): mark 1 (25Ø × 31, top), 2 (20Ø × 31, bottom), 3 / 3a (20Ø @135 side faces), 5 / 5a (20Ø @175 stirrups). Longitudinal bars (1, 2, 3, 3a) take their length from the 25,400 cap length; stirrups (5, 5a) take theirs from the 3400 × 2500 section.

Step 3 — Compute each cutting length (full arithmetic in the next Tutorial section): mark 1 → 29,025 · mark 2 → 28,225 · marks 3/3a → 27,250 · mark 5 → 11,600 · mark 5a → 5,600 mm.

Step 4 — Multiply by bar counts and add up. That is exactly what the table below does live: ≈ 3,633 m total bar length ≈ 10.2 t of steel for the Section C-C bars.

Step 5 — Verify the assumptions. The yellow counts (stirrup sets, side-bar numbers, link widths) are placeholders until the sheet-10 zone dimensions are entered — the total moves with them. White cells are read straight off the drawing.
📝 Tutorial — full worked example, step by step
Example 1 — Stirrup mark 5 (20Ø closed stirrup), exactly as the tool computes it:
1. From page 1 (View B-B): pier cap section at the pier = 3400 wide × 2500 deep. Clear cover 45 mm (note 5).
2. Stirrup outer dimensions: A = 3400 − 2×45 = 3310; B = 2500 − 2×45 = 2410.
3. Shape length = 2(A+B) = 2 × (3310+2410) = 11,440.
4. Hooks: 2 × max(10×20, 75) = 2 × 200 = +400.
5. Bend deductions: 3 corners @90° = 3×2×20 = 120, plus 2 hook bends @135° = 2×3×20 = 120 → −240.
6. Cutting length = 11,440 + 400 − 240 = 11,600 mm (already a multiple of 25, so rounding changes nothing).
7. Weight each = 11.600 m × 2.47 kg/m (IS 1786, Ø20) = 28.65 kg. × 17 stirrups = 487 kg — the row's Weight cell.

Example 2 — Top bar mark 1 (25Ø), showing laps and payable weight:
1. Run along cap = 25,400 − 2×45 = 25,310; two 650 end bends (shape U): 25,310+650+650 = 26,610.
2. Deduct 2 bends @90° = 2×2×25 = −100 → pre-lap length = 26,510.
3. Longer than 12,000 stock → laps needed. Lap = 50×25 = 1,250. Pieces = 1 + ⌈(26,510−12,000)÷(12,000−1,250)⌉ = 3 → 2 laps = +2,500 (that's the 2* in the row).
4. 26,510 + 2,500 = 29,010 → round up to 25 → 29,025 mm cutting length.
5. Weight = 29.025 × 3.85 (IS 1786, Ø25) = 111.75 kg each × 31 bars = 3,464 kg (procurement).
6. Payable (MoRTH 1608 — laps excluded): 26,510 → 26,525 rounded → × 31 × 3.85 = 3,166 kg. That ~300 kg difference is steel the contractor buys but is never paid for.

Bar counts from spacing: bars @135 c/c over a 910 zone = ⌈910÷135⌉ + 1 = 8 per face.

Now try it yourself: change cover in Example 1 (edit A to 3300 for 50 mm cover) and watch the cutting length drop by 20 mm. Every cell recalculates live — that's the whole point.
Lap factors for THIS project (35d / 50d) and IRC:112
Drawing note 6 says lap = 35d, and 50d for bar mark 1 (top bars). This matches IRC:112-2020 exactly: for Fe500D in M60, basic anchorage k = 25 (Table 15.4); with 50% bars lapped at a section ×1.4 → 35d (favourable bond); top bars of a deep pour are "unfavourable bond" ×1.43 → ≈50d. So use 50 in the Lap×dia cell for top bars, 35 for the rest — already prefilled.
Bars over 32 mm must not be lap-spliced (IRC:112 Cl. 15.2.6) — the tool warns and you should use couplers (set Laps = 0 and note "couplers" in Remarks; couplers add no length).
Payable weight vs procurement weight (MoRTH billing)
MoRTH Specification Cl. 1608: reinforcement is paid by measured length including hooks but EXCLUDING laps, converted to tonnes at nominal mass. Wastage, laps, couplers, chairs and binding wire are NOT paid — they are inside the rate.
• Payable weight (green card) = cutting length minus lap lengths × count × unit mass. Use this for BOQ/billing.
• Procurement weight (blue card) = full cutting length incl. laps × count × unit mass, plus the wastage % and rolling-margin % from Settings. Use this for ordering steel.
Rolling margin: steel is billed between supplier and contractor on actual section weight; IS 1786 allows ±3% (bars >16 mm), ±5% (12–16), ±7% (≤10). Enter your measured margin % (negative = rolls light) to adjust the procurement tonnage.
💰 Price calculation — worked example
Two different money numbers come out of the same BBS, and confusing them is how contractors lose money:

1. What you PAY the steel supplier (procurement cost)
= procurement weight × purchase rate.
Procurement weight = total cutting length incl. laps × unit mass, + wastage %, ± rolling margin %.
With this table: 10,216 kg + 3% wastage = 10,522 kg. At ₹62/kg → ≈ ₹6,52,000 (the blue card computes this live once you enter your rate).

2. What the client PAYS YOU (item-rate/BOQ contract, MoRTH Cl. 1608)
= payable weight × BOQ rate. Payable weight excludes laps, wastage, binding wire, chairs — here 9,672 kg. At the same ₹62 → ≈ ₹5,99,000.

3. The gap is yours to absorb — here ≈ 850 kg ≈ ₹53,000 on these bars alone: lap steel (~544 kg), wastage (~306 kg), plus binding wire (figure ~7–13 kg per tonne) and chairs. A BOQ rate must be quoted high enough to swallow this; that's why lap counts and the cutting plan matter financially.

Rolling margin: suppliers bill by actual weighbridge weight, but bars rarely weigh exactly d²/162. Weigh 1 m samples per diameter per lot; if they run 2% light, enter −2 in Settings → the tool buys 2% fewer kg for the same metres (IS 1786 allows ±3% for bars over 16 mm). Whoever ignores rolling margin gifts that percentage to the other party.

Cutting plan savings: the optimizer at the bottom shows waste % per diameter. Every 1% of waste avoided on this cap ≈ 100 kg ≈ ₹6,200 — usually the difference between cutting bars in a planned sequence vs ad-hoc.
Unit weight
Default uses the IS 1786 Table 1 nominal masses (8:0.395, 10:0.617, 12:0.885, 16:1.58, 20:2.47, 25:3.85, 28:4.83, 32:6.31, 36:7.99, 40:9.86 kg/m) — the values billing must use. Untick the toggle to fall back to d²/162 for odd diameters.
Shape library formulas
Straight: A · L-bend: A+B (1×90°) · U / end-bends: A+B+C (2×90°) · Crank/bent-up: A + 2×0.42D (4×45°, the 0.42D rule) · Closed 2-leg stirrup: 2(A+B) + 2 hooks (3×90° + 2×135°) · Circular ring: πD + 2 hooks (2×135°) · Spiral/helix: n×√((πD)² + P²) + 2πD closing turns, n = ⌈H/P⌉+1 · Chair: A+2B+2C (4×90°) · Custom: comma-separated segment lengths + your own bend counts.
For stirrups measure A, B out-to-out: member size − 2×cover (this cap: 3400−90=3310, 2500−90=2410 at the pier).
Bars are counted as members × (⌈run ÷ spacing⌉ + 1) when Spacing and Run are filled; otherwise members × bars/member typed directly.
Cutting plan (steel ordering)
The optimizer packs every cut piece (per diameter) into stock bars using first-fit-decreasing — the same heuristic commercial cut-and-bend software uses, typically within 1–3% of the mathematical optimum. Offcuts longer than the reuse threshold are counted reusable, not waste. Bars longer than stock are split at laps automatically before nesting.

📘 Guide — how to use & make changes (no coding needed)

First time / checking the P355 numbers
0. Prefer watching it live? Press the green ▶ Tutorial button under the bars table — it builds a stirrup in front of you, filling every value step by step and ending where the result appears.
1. Every white cell was read from drawing SPS-P355-VIA-SD; every yellow cell is an assumption — fix those first.
2. From sheet 10 plan, find the stirrup zone lengths: type each zone's length into the Run cell of bars 5 / 5a — the bar count then computes itself from the @175 spacing (the Bars/mem cell locks automatically when Run + Spacing are filled).
3. From Section C-C, count/measure the 5a internal link widths and replace the assumed 300 in dimension A.
4. Side bars 3 / 3a: enter each zone depth in Run (spacing 135 is already set, Members = 2 faces).
5. Add the missing bars (3b, 4, 4a, M1/M2 mesh) with + Add bar when you have sheets 7–11.
Choosing from the Shape dropdown
Match the bar's sketch in the drawing's shape column to a preset — the dropdown then shows only the dimensions that shape needs, applies the right bend deductions automatically, and pre-fills the usual hooks:
• Plain bar, no bends → Straight (just A).
• One end bent 90° (L shape) → L-bend (A = long leg, B = bent leg).
• Both ends bent 90° — like bars 1, 3, 3a here (long bar with 650 or 300 downstands) → U / end-bends (A = run, B and C = the two end legs).
• Bar stepping up/down at 45° (bent-up slab bar, column crank) → Crank (A = total run, D = crank depth; the 0.42D rule is applied for you).
• Closed rectangle with hook marks — stirrups 5 and 5a here → Closed stirrup (A × B out-to-out = member size − 2×cover; 2 hooks + 3×90° + 2×135° automatic).
• Round link in a circular pier/pile → Circular ring (D = core diameter); continuous coil → Spiral/helix (D, pitch P, height H).
• Support stool between top and bottom mats → Chair (A = head, B = height, C = legs).
• Anything else — like bar 2 here (bottom bar following the tapered soffit) → Custom segments: type each leg length comma-separated and set the bend counts yourself.
Switching shape keeps the rest of the row (dia, spacing, counts, laps) — only the dimension fields change. The sketch redraws instantly so you can see you picked right.
Changing a rule (lap, hook, wastage, rate…)
• All rules live in Settings — hover a label to read what it controls. Nothing is hidden in code.
• Per-bar lap rule: the Lap ×dia cell on each row (50 for top mark 1, 35 for others, per drawing note 6).
• Project-specific hook rule? Change Hook length ×dia (10 → 6 for IS 13920:2016, 8 for non-seismic 90° hooks).
• Steel price: put your rate in ₹/kg and the procurement card shows cost.
• Different structure entirely? Change the Project header, delete rows, add your own — the engine is generic.
📄 Read PDF (beta) — what it can and can't do
Upload the drawing PDF and the tool reads its text layer: the reinforcement table (bar marks, diameters, counts, spacing, shape hints like 2L/12L) and the notes (clear cover, lap rules, concrete/steel grade). You get a review screen first — nothing applies until you tick and confirm, and every applied value lands yellow with a "PDF-read — verify" remark.
It cannot read dimensions off the drawing geometry (which line is 3400 vs 2500), bar shapes, or zone extents — those still come from your eyes. A future 🤖 AI-read upgrade is planned for that. Scanned/photographed PDFs have no text layer and won't work. This feature needs internet once (to load the PDF reader library); everything else in the tool stays offline.

Reading the results
• Cut len = one bar's cutting length in mm (already rounded up to 25 mm). Weight includes laps (what you buy); Payable excludes laps (what MoRTH pays).
• Laps shown with * were inserted automatically because the bar is longer than 12 m stock.
• ⚠ marks a problem — hover it to read (e.g. lap on a >32 mm bar, bar longer than stock with laps forced to 0).
• The Cutting plan at the bottom says exactly how many 12 m bars to order per diameter and how to cut each one.
